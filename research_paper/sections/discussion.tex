\paragraph{What a defense for derived state has to buy.}
Once read-time checks are in place, zero leakage is cheap: a per-user epoch reaches it with a few lines
of code. A finer mechanism has to earn its place on what it lets the user keep. \pending{D2: state
the measured utility gap between epoch, flush, full and full\_fine, short and long sessions, and
whether the gap justifies taint.} Sentence-level taint relies on exact attribution, which our stub
provides and real models do not; context attribution~\cite{cohenwang2024contextcite} and permissive
information-flow analysis~\cite{siddiqui2024permissive} are the candidate routes, and each needs its
own soundness argument.

\paragraph{Shared state turns re-disclosure into new disclosure.}
Re-showing a user what it already saw gives it nothing new, but the same mechanism writes revoked
content into state that other users read: a tenant-shared cache served restricted answers to insiders
who never had access, and laundered text reaches colleagues through whatever cache the defense still
shares. \pending{D3: colleague numbers from R7.}

\paragraph{Metadata and content are separate problems.}
With a pre-filter, the score and reranker channels carry no signal because the target is invisible in
both worlds; what remains is shared state and timing. \pending{D4: post-filter residual channels with
honest scores; timing residual with real models and bucket padding.}

\paragraph{Practical guidance, limited to what was measured.}
Scope semantic caches per user, or check every hit against live authorization. Enforce revocation on
read against live identity state rather than waiting for propagation. Bound chat-session retention.
Filter by ACL before scoring, and never expose scores of withheld candidates. Do not add a fast
refusal path without padding. Budget separately for users who re-type what they already read.
